# History of the Known World

**Type:** world event
**Tags:** history, old empire, quarack, aurelium, cataclysm, new empire, seven heroes, magic, worm

---

## Entry

The world as it stands today was shaped by three great forces: the expansion of an ancient southern civilization, a catastrophe that drowned two centuries in darkness, and the fragile empire built atop the ashes by heroes who paid a price no one yet fully understands.

---

## The Southern Island — Aurelium & the Quarack Civilization

Before the continent was mapped, before the North Island was reached, civilization flourished on a distant southern island now known as **Aurelium**. Its people, the **Quarack**, built something rarely seen in the world — an enlightened, prosperous culture comparable to Ancient Greece at its peak. They were scholars, architects, navigators, and philosophers. Trade, craft, and scholarship defined them.

At some point, the Quarack looked north. Their ships crossed dark waters and found a vast continent populated by peoples mired in what the Quarack would have recognized as the Dark Ages — small fortress-towns, fragmented kingdoms, subsistence survival. Where the Quarack had light, the continent had shadow.

The Quarack did not hesitate. Expansion began.

---

## The Conquest of the Continent

The Quarack moved northward across the continent over generations. The native peoples — though proud and resilient — could not match Quarack organization, tactics, or technology. Fortress-towns fell. Alliances were absorbed. A new hybrid culture began to emerge wherever the Quarack settled: their sophistication blending with the raw, gothic character of the continent's people.

But expansion had limits.

To the **west**, the Quarack encountered the **Darro Empire** — a civilization of dragon-like beings, older and more advanced than even themselves. Wisely, they did not press further. The western border hardened into a permanent boundary by unspoken mutual agreement.

To the **east**, vast dark forests gave way to mountains, and beyond the mountains lived **barbarian tribes** — fierce, mobile, and uninterested in diplomacy. The Quarack pushed them back toward the peaks but never fully subdued them. The barbarians retreated deeper into the mountain foothills and remained a persistent eastern pressure.

With west and east checked, the Quarack consolidated their hold on the central continent. What emerged was an empire in the Roman tradition — aqueducts, roads, administrative systems, cultural fusion. It was known simply as the **Old Empire**. Its true name, if it had one beyond the continent, has been forgotten by all who live here now.

---

## The North Island — Three Peoples, One Border

Quarack ambition eventually looked further north still, to a large island vaguely resembling a distant cold Britain. They established a **colony in the island's southern zone**, bringing with them continental culture, infrastructure, and law.

The island, however, was already home to more than wilderness. Its three zones became three distinct peoples:

- **South** — the Quarack colonial zone, governed from the continent, culturally dominant.
- **Middle** — **Halnazar**, a kingdom of people who had arrived from somewhere else entirely — further east, further unknown — with a culture closer to ancient Asian traditions. They had been trying to survive long before the Quarack arrived.
- **North** — tribal warriors, a blend of Norse and ancient Slavic culture, raiding southward seasonally.

Halnazar found itself trapped: Old Empire expansion pressing from the south, northern raiders hammering from above. Their solution was grim and practical. They built **Great Wall-style fortifications** on both borders. And when walls alone were not enough, they turned to **necromancy**. The fallen — their own dead, enemy dead, anyone who had stopped breathing — were raised to continue serving. Labor, defense, war. Death in Halnazar was not an ending; it was a reassignment.

This culture of pragmatic necromancy endures. Its current ruler is **Xau-Injan**, the Lich-king — himself a product of this philosophy taken to its logical extreme.

---

## The Old Empire at Its Height

For centuries, the Old Empire grew. What began as Greek-minded conquest became something Roman in character, and then — slowly, unmistakably — began tipping toward Renaissance. Early firearms appeared. Libraries multiplied. The population of the continent swelled under relative peace. The Quarack of Aurelium and the peoples of the continent had blended into something new.

Then the sky fell.

---

## The Cataclysm

A meteorite struck the eastern mountains — the barbarian foothills, beyond the edge of imperial control. What emerged from the crater was not rock or fire.

It was the **Heart of Darkness**: an Old Dark God, a rival and enemy to the Worm and the Unspeakable, now cracked open and bleeding into the world.

Monsters poured from the impact site in waves. A **strange light** appeared in the sky — not sun, not moon — and wherever it touched, people changed. Mutations spread. Madness followed. The eastern frontier collapsed first. Then the interior. Libraries burned. Cities emptied. The population of the continent fell by numbers no one recorded because no one was left to count.

This period is called the **Age of Darkness**. It lasted **two hundred years**.

During this time, **Aurelium sealed itself off**. The southern island — home of the Quarack, cradle of the civilization that had built the Old Empire — destroyed every ship that approached its shores. No explanation was given. No aid was sent. They simply disappeared behind their own silence.

The name of the Old Empire was forgotten. The people of the continent called it nothing, because they had stopped remembering.

---

## The Seven Heroes

Near the end of the second century of darkness, seven individuals from the shattered remnants of the continent's peoples gathered with a single purpose: reach the eastern mountains, find the Heart of Darkness, and destroy it.

What is known publicly is that they succeeded. What is not publicly known is the cost.

To face a god, one requires power beyond mortal reach. The seven found it — or it found them. **The Yellow**, a force born from the partnership of the Worm and the Unspeakable, offered them what they needed. They accepted.

The Yellow's gift is not a blessing. It is a **slow consumption** — a corruption that burns through the body and soul like a fire that never quite goes out. The Yellow can slow this process. The Worm can even reverse it temporarily. But neither can cure it. To carry the Yellow's power is to be perpetually dying, perpetually dependent on the Worm's mercy to remain alive.

The seven descended into the mountains. They fought the Heart of Darkness.

**The seventh hero did not survive the battle.** In the final moment, they sacrificed themselves — willingly, deliberately — channeling everything the Yellow had given them and everything they were into a single act of annihilation. The Heart of Darkness was destroyed.

And from that sacrifice, something unexpected was born: **magic entered the world**. The seventh hero's consumed soul seeded arcane power into reality itself. Every mage who has ever cast a spell, every scholar who has ever shaped the unseen — they do so because of this death. Magic is, at its root, sacrifice. The seventh hero is never named in any surviving record. Whether this is coincidence or design, no one can say.

---

## The New Empire & the Three Emperors

Six heroes walked out of the mountains.

From those six, **three emperors** were chosen to rule what would become the **New Empire** — raised from the ruins of the Old Empire on the continent:

- **The Holy Emperor** — religious authority
- **The State Emperor** — civil governance
- **The Warfare Emperor** — military command

The New Empire presents itself as the world reborn: civilization restored, darkness defeated, order returned. Its three emperors are celebrated as saviors.

What the empire does not advertise is that all three emperors still carry the Yellow inside them. They are still burning. They are still dependent on the Worm's patience to stay alive. The New Empire was not built by free people — it was built by three individuals who cannot afford to refuse the Worm anything.

The heroism was real. The sacrifice was real. The founding of the New Empire was real.

The chains are also real.

## What Remains Unknown

- The true name of the Old Empire, if it had one.
- What Aurelium knows, and why they sealed themselves away.
- Whether the seventh hero's sacrifice was chosen freely, or arranged.
- What the Worm intends to do with three emperors and a world full of mages born from the power it helped create.
- What the Heart of Darkness was trying to achieve before it was destroyed — and whether it succeeded at something before the end.
